Getting started with Stitch

Stitch brings oil and gas field data from several datasets into one record per field. It shows where each value came from, and lets you correct, enrich and de-duplicate the data, with a record of who did what and why.

Signing in and what you can see

Open Stitch and click Log in to continue. Your account decides what you see:

  • Data. You only see values from datasets you're licensed for. Without a WoodMac licence, WoodMac values never appear for you, on any page.
  • Tabs. Everyone gets Resources. Depending on your role you may also see Entity linkage, Merge review and ETL pipelines.
  • Actions. Editing values, AI suggestions and approving merges are switched on per account. If a button you need is missing or gives a permission error, ask the Stitch team.

Find a field

  • Search looks in name, local name, basin, state/province and region. It doesn't search by country, so use the Country filter for that. Press Enter to run it.
  • Filters: Country, Region, State/Province, Basin, Field status and Primary hydrocarbon group. You can pick several values in each, and the options narrow to match what's already selected.
  • Sort by clicking a column header. Change how many rows show at the bottom of the list.
  • The Data source mix bar shows how much of each record comes from each dataset. Hover over it for counts.
  • Your search and filters stay in the page address, so you can bookmark or share a filtered list.

Read a record

Click a row to open the record. Values are grouped under Identity and location, Organizations and Production and geology.

  • Each value shows its source as a color and a label underneath.
  • Click a value to open All sources: every dataset's answer for that field, with the one in use at the top. Different answers in this list mean the datasets disagree.
  • The Sources section at the bottom lists every dataset attached to the record. Click View to see when it was imported, any curator note, and the original raw record.
  • If you open an old record that has since been merged, Stitch takes you to the merged record and tells you so.

How Stitch picks the value you see

When datasets disagree, the highest-ranked one with a value wins:

  1. RMI (your corrections)
  2. WoodMac
  3. C&C Reservoirs
  4. BC Energy Regulator
  5. Alberta Energy Regulator
  6. Norwegian Offshore Directorate
  7. GEM
  8. LLM (AI suggestions)

So a correction you make always shows, and an AI suggestion only shows where no dataset has a value.

Correct a value

  1. Click the value to open All sources, then click Edit.
  2. Click + and type the New value, or choose one from the list for fields like status or hydrocarbon group.
  3. Add a Note saying where you found it, such as “Operator annual report 2025, p. 14”. It's optional, but the next person will rely on it.
  4. Click Save. Your value is stored as an RMI source, marked curated, and is used from then on.

With access to every dataset, you can also use Edit and the ↑ ↓ arrows to change which dataset wins for that one field.

Ask the AI to fill a gap

  1. On a record, go to AI suggestion, choose a Field and click Generate suggestion. It can take up to half a minute.
  2. Read the reasoning, and open at least one of the cited sources to check it.
  3. If you agree, click Add to resource. If not, leave it. Nothing is saved until you add it.

Getting good results

  • “No grounded suggestion” is common. The AI won't answer without a citation. Trying again sometimes works.
  • Years (FID, production start), basin and state/province work best. Leave state/province alone for offshore fields.
  • The AI works from the data already on the record plus a web search, so records with little data give it less to go on.
  • Enrich this resource asks about 14 fields at once. Expect several empty results.

Review possible duplicates

When two records look like the same field, they become a merge candidate waiting in Merge review.

  1. Pick a candidate from the Queue. Search by name, basin or record ID, and filter by status.
  2. Compare the two side by side. ✓ Match and ≠ Differs mark each row, and Other attributes shows the rest.
  3. Check location, operator and dates. The same name in the same country isn't always the same field.
  4. Write a Decision note: what you checked, and anything you're unsure about.
  5. Click Approve merge or Deny merge. Approving creates one new combined record, and the old records redirect to it. Denying keeps them apart and stops the same pair being proposed again.

Find duplicates automatically

Entity linkage scans every record for likely duplicates. Click Start run. With Initiate merges ticked, each group it finds is added to Merge review; untick it to just see what it would find. It currently matches only records with exactly the same name and country, so it misses pairs like “Agbada” and “Agbada Oil and Gas Field (Nigeria)”.

Words you'll see

Resource
One oil or gas field in Stitch, combining every dataset attached to it.
Source
One dataset's record for that field, such as a WoodMac row or a GEM row. Your corrections and accepted AI suggestions are sources too.
Data source mix
The colored bar showing how much of a record each source supplies.
Curated
A value a person entered by hand, labelled RMI.
Merge candidate
A pair of records that might be the same field, waiting for a person to decide.
ETL pipeline
The import job that loads one dataset into Stitch. Most users never need it.
Something wrong in the data? Correct it with a note, or send the Stitch team the record ID shown under the record's name. Found a bug or have an idea? Open an issue on GitHub.
